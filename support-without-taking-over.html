How to Support Your Child’s Shidduch Journey Without Taking Over

A mother’s heart is deeply invested in her child’s happiness. This guide will help you become a source of strength, clarity, and encouragement.

Download the Guide — free
Yours to keep — save it, print it, or pass it along.
Six chapters

A source of strength, clarity, and encouragement

1

Your Child’s Journey Is Not Your Report Card

Let go of unnecessary guilt. Learn the difference between influence and responsibility.

Reflection: What am I carrying that does not belong to me?

2

Listening Is Often More Powerful Than Advising

Create emotional safety. Listen to understand, not merely to respond.

Reflection: Am I listening to understand, or listening to respond?

3

Becoming a Messenger of Hope

Replace fear-based communication with hope-based communication.

Reflection: What message does my child hear most often from me?

4

When to Speak and When to Stay Quiet

Learn to distinguish between support and anxiety-driven involvement.

Reflection: Am I helping because my child needs it, or because my anxiety needs relief?

5

Start Earlier, Not Later

Build communication, self-awareness, and resilience before a crisis develops.

6

Five Things Your Child Needs From You

Belief, Patience, Listening, Encouragement, and Perspective.
